## Competition Strategy

For comp, you will want to get the sub in the water ASAP, the earlier the better. Preferably 6 months before comp begins you want to begin water testing.

8 months before comp, you should already be updating the simulator and running simulation runs to make sure the software is working well. This polishes up your code so that in real-life pool tests, your energy can be devoted to validating approaches instead of fixing stupid syntax bugs or something like that.

For tasks to do, you only need to do 3 tasks to make it into finals, which is around 2-3k points. For example, you could do gate, buoy, octagon surface, or gate, buoy, one bin drop to make it to finals.

If you can reliably do 3 tasks, you will probably get within top 5.

However, if you want to be #1, there is always usually one team that is cracked and can do every task. 

For example, in comp of 2022, doing gate, buoy, and octagon surface (detecting stools with front camera, aligning with down camera with middle stool) got us into the finals as the 4th seed. We then added a bin drop in the finals, dropping 2 markers (downContinuousAlign), getting us 2nd place. However, BumbleBee (National University of Singapore) was absolutely cracked and did every single task perfectly and quickly. So, to beat them we would have needed to be able to do every single task faster than them. Or, have two subs and get inter vehicle communication points. 

So, if you want to be #1, you have to have a sub that can do every task, including gate, buoy, bin, torpedoes, octagon (with grabbers). To test this, you will need a good mechanical design, and you will also need to be testing a sht ton, with as many simulator runs and in person pool testing with props as you can to lock in each task. You also need to have very efficient mission code so that you can complete these tasks quick enough under the time limit. As soon as the sub hits the water from the harness, your competition run time starts to count down. 

I can't understate this enough: start early, as early as possible!!!! Comp hits you like a brick and comes around the corner faster than you think. Start with why, your reason why you want to go to comp/compete, and that will lead you and motivate you. It will certainly be hard, and there will be times when you want to quit. Several times where you want to quit. It all comes down to if your why is strong enough to bear the storm and continue trudging on. Humility and growth mindset is important to continue learning and improving. 

And if your systems are getting more complex, that's when you need to have God-tier documentation so it doesn't get lost over the years, causing the club to stall. Documentation is very important.  

### What Comp is Like

Comp is crazy man. In 2022 I got average of 3 hours of sleep. One night we pulled a pretty much all nighter labeling images. The ML god Kush Nayak pulled 2 straight all nighters training ML models lmao. 

You will be shuttling around all the time, staying up late fixing issues and labeling images, and waking up early to go to pool test slots. Make sure you have thought everything through and prepared everything you need to prepare. 

Try to pre-qualify; it will help a sht ton and reduce lots of stress as there will probably be some problem that comes up during practice/qualification runs. 

On the days of semi-final runs, you just have to be on-edge and paranoid. Make a checklist of every single thing you need to check, and run through it: mech, electrical, and software checklists. Before the run on the dock, run systems check of as many components as you can, such as the microcontroller, computer, cameras, AHRS, pressure sensors, etc. Basically run as many checks as humanly possible. Then, pre-load your code onto the sub while on land, then untether, put the sub in the water, and have them unkill. This saves time instead of having to tether in while the sub is in the water and start the code. Also turn on image logging for all runs. It helps a ton to see what happens and gives you extra training data if needed if the model performed not as well as you would have liked (happened a couple times to us in 2022.)

You will need to bring hella software people to be able to have enough image labellers. Before you go to comp, make sure you have as many tasks tested as possible in the sim and in the real life pool. Also bring extra, spares of as many components as you can. 

Meet as many teams as you can, and learn as much as you can. Give it your best effort and do your best, and that's all you can control. The rest is up to the universe. 

You might get recruited by people like the Navy, or university acoustic laboratories; it's up to you if you want to accept or not. Accepting those offers for internships is crazy cool, but also you won't have enough time for AVBotz, or enough time to make AVBotz good. If you don't have enough time to dedicate to Botz, Botz will perform mid. AVBotz requires as much time as you can possibly put into it.

### Other

Also try to make sure that you talk in public channels as much as possible. It breathes life into the club and helps get everyone involved and feeling like they are part of a team. Restricting conversations to DMs creates a stifling atmosphere where underclassmen feel excluded. 

Also try to be familiar with a terminal editor, like Vim (can learn through Vimtutor, search that up) or Nano. This will come in handy when comp comes where you have to ssh into the sub all the time. SSH is preferable to VNC because VNC is hella slow sometimes. 

Also work a specified amount every week. 3 hours of work per week (actually time it) should be your minimum (meeting times don't count, 3 hours of work outside of meetings.)

Also the simulator is hella useful. If you can get things working in the simulator you're 70% there. That means the code's foundation is good, you just need to ensure the hardware (sensors, motors, etc.) are working irl.

Also when admitting new members focus on character/attitude. People with good character and attitude have high potential of succeeding, whereas people who are smart but have not good character can cause more harm to the club than good.
